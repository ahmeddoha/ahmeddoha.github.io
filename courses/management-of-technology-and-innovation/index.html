One decision discipline across the term

Prepare the evidence. Argue the alternatives. Commit to a course of action.

Every case puts you in the decision-maker’s seat. Your job is not to retell the story; it is to diagnose the decision, use the exhibits and theory, and defend what should happen next.

The Case Rhythm

The same analytical sequence powers case discussions, presentations, reports, and the platform project.

Step 1

Prepare + diagnose

Read the case and assigned theory. Separate the decision from its symptoms, identify the evidence, and rank the issues.

You arrive with a position worth testing.
→
Step 2

Analyze + choose

Apply the right framework at the right level, work the exhibits, compare plausible alternatives, and state decision criteria.

You leave description behind and make a choice.
→
Step 3

Defend + implement

Test the recommendation under challenge, trace second-order effects, and specify who does what, when, and with what stop condition.

You turn an opinion into an executable strategy.

The three-module arc

Understand how innovation moves—then decide how a firm should move.

The eight cases are organized across three modules: 3 + 2 + 3. AI Wars in 2025 is the third case in Module 3, where the course turns from creating value to capturing it.

The evidence rule

A recommendation earns its force from the chain beneath it.

Begin with the decision. Choose a framework because it fits the question and level of analysis. Use the exhibits and primary company disclosure. Compare realistic alternatives. Then recommend and implement.

If the number, assumption, or causal link cannot survive a question from the other team, the strategy is not ready.

1DecisionWhat must the manager decide now?
2Framework + evidenceWhich theory fits, and what do the case exhibits or company disclosures establish?
3Alternatives + criteriaWhat can the firm realistically do, and how will you choose?
4Recommendation + implementationWhat happens first, who owns it, what breaks, and when should the firm stop?
